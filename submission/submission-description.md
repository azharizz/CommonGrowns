# CommonGrown

## Your team just talks. The agent finds the deal.

**CommonGrown** is an AI opportunity network that lives in your team chat. Employees talk in their company group as usual. An agent on **Switch** reads along, keeps a short summary of what the company needs and offers (not the chat), asks the owner before publishing anything, then finds the right partner, sponsor or client on a shared exchange and prepares both sides. Then it steps back, because **humans close the deal**.

The name comes from *common ground*: two companies, each rooted in its own private soil, growing toward the same deal.

## Inspiration

Picture a tech summit in Jakarta: five thousand people expected, zero sponsors signed. In Singapore, a cloud company is about to launch in Indonesia, and nobody there knows its name.

They need each other, and they will probably never meet.

That is how B2B deals still start: by luck, through referrals, events and cold outreach. Then the first meetings are spent on basic qualification, because neither side knows what the other really needs.

The strange part is that the need is already written down. It is in the company's team chat, where people plan, argue about budgets and joke about lunch. Nobody will copy that into a form. And nobody should expose it either: right next to the need sits the number the company will not go under.

So we asked: could an agent read along in the chat a team already uses, understand the need, and bring back the right counterpart, without leaking what must stay private? That needs an agent that truly lives in the room with people. That is what Switch is for.

| **6 · Prepares the meeting** | A paid brief gives risks, what is still unknown, a pitch angle and a next step. | The first meeting starts from the deal, not the basics. |

## How Switch is used

**Switch is the whole connection between the team and the agent.** Our worker has no chat code. It holds one WebSocket to Switch, and every message from every company's group arrives through it.

| Switch provides | We would otherwise build | CommonGrown adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chat bridge:** one bot, a room per group | A bot and connection per chat app | Listening and folding chat into facts |
| **Rooms:** one agent serves every company, each isolated | Tenant routing | One company per group, with an owner |
| **Identity:** `sender`, `sender_name` on every event | Per-app user lookup | Owner-only approval and credit spend |
| **Addressing:** `addressed` on every event | Per-app mention parsing | Commands when asked, silence otherwise |
| **Message log:** a cursor to resume from | A store and replay logic | Replay-safe work after a reconnect |

A person and an agent finish a real task together in the chat: the team supplies the context, the agent drafts and matches, and the owner's two yeses are the human decision the workflow cannot pass without.

## How the agent works

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/azharizz/CommonGrowns/main/submission/assets/commongrown-loop.gif" alt="Animated timeline of who does what: team talks, agent learns and asks, owner says yes twice, agent matches, both sides close" width="100%">
</p>

1. **Listen.** The worker connects with `filter=all`, so it receives ordinary team talk, not only mentions. Raw text is buffered briefly (24 hours, 40 messages per room).
2. **Learn.** Every few messages the model folds the buffer into facts: need, offer, constraint or fact, each public or private, with the source shown only as `owner`, `member N` or `team`. The raw messages are then deleted. A newer statement replaces an older one; contradictions are kept for the owner.
3. **Ask.** When the facts show an unpublished need, the agent asks. A first yes builds a draft from public facts only, showing where each line came from.
4. **Publish.** A second yes writes fixed public fields to the exchange. Never on one yes.
5. **Match.** Chroma recalls by meaning in both directions, with hard filters on status and budget overlap. The model ranks the top 4 and explains four fits, risks and missing information.
6. **Brief.** Headlines are free. A brief spends a credit atomically, and a failed generation refunds it.

| The agent can | The agent cannot |
| --- | --- |
| Read along in a linked group and keep a structured summary. | Keep the chat itself. Raw messages are deleted once summarized. |
| Draft an opportunity from public facts, with sources. | Publish on one yes, or approve its own draft. |
| Rank candidates and explain fit, with a clear “no match”. | Put a private floor or ceiling in a draft, a match or another company's view. |
| Flag a contradiction for the owner. | Settle a disagreement by guessing. |

### What stays private

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/azharizz/CommonGrowns/main/submission/assets/commongrown-privacy.gif" alt="Real demo chat lines are sorted: the package and public range rise into a public record with fixed fields, the $40k floor falls below the line, the lunch joke dissolves" width="100%">
</p>

The public record has fixed fields, and none of them is for private notes. So a floor cannot be written to the exchange by mistake. That is a code-level gate, not a prompt rule. It is a trusted-broker design, not cryptography, and we say so.

## How we make money

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/azharizz/CommonGrowns/main/submission/assets/film-money.gif" alt="A price staircase rises out of the soil: free headlines, 1 credit full brief, 2 credits private budget check, 3 credits verified brief, then NO CUT OF THE DEAL" width="80%">
</p>

We charge for what only a neutral broker can do. A meeting can move to Zoom or WhatsApp without us, so we don't charge per introduction or take a cut of the deal: we couldn't see it or attribute it. We charge where value lands before the meeting and can't be copied outside.

| Output | Cost | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Match headline** | Free | Company, verdict, one line why |
| **Full brief** | 1 credit | Four fits, risks, unknowns, pitch angle |
| **Private budget check** | 2 credits | Overlap or not, **never the numbers** |
| **Verified brief** | 3 credits | The full brief plus the counterpart's verification level |

The private check is the moat: it needs both sides' hidden numbers, and only a neutral party can hold them. Later: annual plans and white-label private networks for communities and conference series. Credit costs are built; money prices are not set.

## How we built it

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/azharizz/CommonGrowns/main/submission/assets/commongrown-architecture.gif" alt="Animated architecture: a message travels from Telegram through Switch over a WebSocket to the CommonGrown worker, which uses SQLite, Chroma and the OpenAI Agents SDK, and the reply travels back" width="100%">

| Technology | Role in CommonGrown |
| --- | --- |
| **Switch** | Self-hosted on Docker (core, gateway, Postgres): chat bridge, rooms, identity, addressing and message log. |
| **Python 3.11–3.13 · websockets** | One worker with one serial queue, reconnect and checkpoint, “working on it” notices. Serves every company group. |
| **OpenAI Agents SDK** | Scoped tools and validated output, against any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. No model is hardcoded. |
| **Chroma · all-MiniLM-L6-v2** | Two documents per opportunity. Forward and reverse recall, with hard filters. |
| **SQLite** | Accounts, atomic credits, groups, facts, conflicts, consent, the short evidence buffer. |
| **GCP** | One `e2-small` VM. No public app port, no reserved IP, IAP-only SSH. |

## Challenges we ran into

- **Listening without hoarding.** An agent that reads everything could easily become a chat archive. We made raw text a short-lived buffer that exists only as input for the next summary, and stored structured facts instead.
- **Privacy that doesn't depend on a prompt.** “Please don't reveal the floor” is not a control. We removed the place a floor could go: the public record has fixed fields, and private facts never enter the drafting prompt.
- **Knowing when to speak.** In listen mode the agent sees everything, so it must stay quiet almost all the time. Switch's `addressed` flag separates commands from ordinary talk, and the agent replies to unaddressed chatter only to raise a need or act on a yes.
- **Its own echo.** A bot that listens to everything also hears itself. We had to treat the agent's display name as its own when skipping self-messages.
- **Slow steps in a fast chat.** Matching takes 40 to 70 seconds. “Working on it” notices keep the chat honest about that, and a serial queue keeps one company's work from colliding with another's.
- **A public demo that can't be abused.** Anyone can join the demo groups, so we let members act there while capping model-heavy requests at 25 per group per day.

## Accomplishments we're proud of

- Five live Telegram groups, one shared agent, five different outcomes, including honest weak fits and “no match” results.
- The same company failing as a sponsor and succeeding as an implementation partner: matching on opportunities, not profiles.
- A two-yes consent flow with every drafted line traced to who said it.
- Credits that are atomic and replay-safe, with refunds when generation fails.
- A 2:58 showreel made from the real run, with an original funk-rock score composed in code and a locally generated voice.

## What we learned

The most useful thing an agent can do in a team chat is mostly nothing: listen, keep the point, and speak only when it has something worth a person's yes. The hard engineering is in the boundaries: what may be kept, what may leave the group, and which decisions must stay human.

Switch changed how we scoped the project. With chat, rooms, identity and addressing already solved, all our effort went into the broker logic that is actually the product.

## What's next

- Test the Slack, Discord, Teams and Mattermost bridges, which should need no new connector.
- Run the deal-room commands live: mutual interest, shared positions and meeting prep.

## Honest limits

- **Telegram only, live.** The other chat apps are experimental and not tested.
- **Trusted broker, not cryptography.** The platform can read stored facts. The bot itself tells teams not to post confidential numbers.
- **Fictional demo.** The companies and deals are demo data. There are no real customers and no measured savings.
- **No real payments or verification.** Money prices are not set, verification levels are demo flags, and willingness to pay is unvalidated.
- **Not shown live.** The private budget check, verified brief and deal-room commands are implemented and unit-tested only.
- **The Off test in the film is an illustration,** not a recorded outage.
- **Known limits.** One opportunity per company, no check on company names at `/setup`, and the budget filter can exclude a good match.
